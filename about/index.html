About Guild Atelier

Built on time actually spent inside these problems.

Guild Atelier is an independent consulting and communication practice based in Vietnam, working across cross-cultural, hospitality, and communication challenges in multinational environments. The approach is practical rather than theoretical: start with what's actually happening in a specific organization, then work out what to do about it.

The Through-Line

Different settings. The same kinds of questions.

Manufacturing floors, service environments, classrooms, and meetings between offices that don't quite understand each other may look like completely different problems. In practice, they keep raising many of the same questions:

  • What was supposed to happen, and what actually happened?
  • What did people understand, and what did they only assume?
  • What made one behavior easier than another?
  • What happens between the boxes on the formal process?

There's often a gap between what a process says should happen and what people actually have to do to make the work happen. That gap is usually where the real problem lives: a quality failure, a service breakdown, a misunderstood instruction, two colleagues who each think the other agreed to something different. Guild Atelier repeatedly looks at what happens when people, expectations, communication, processes, and systems meet in actual work. Different settings reveal different versions of the same underlying problem, which is why cross-cultural consulting, hospitality consulting, and communication work all belong under one roof here.

Experience

Experience across the work itself

My background is in operations. I spent years working in manufacturing at Boeing, followed later by food safety and quality management in food manufacturing. Those were environments where procedures mattered, but so did understanding what actually happened when procedures met real people, real constraints, and real production pressure. That's where I learned to pay attention to the gap between what a system is designed to do and what it actually does under real conditions.

I also spent years living and working in Japan, where apparently familiar workplaces often ran on assumptions very different from the ones I brought with me. That experience taught me to be careful about surface similarity, and to keep checking what people actually mean, expect, and understand rather than assuming I already knew.

I live and work in Vietnam now, in Ho Chi Minh City, where that same habit of checking assumptions shapes Guild Atelier's cross-cultural and executive consulting work.

I've also spent time teaching, working directly with English learners and talking with other teachers about what actually helps someone build usable ability, not just knowledge. That's a different version of the same question that shows up everywhere else in this work: what can someone actually do, as opposed to what they theoretically know?

Small by design.

Guild Atelier is small by design. John is directly involved in every engagement, from the first conversation through the work itself. There's no standard package applied before understanding the actual situation, and no framework introduced because a slide deck calls for one.

The starting point is always the specific problem in front of a specific organization: what's actually happening, who's involved, and what each of them is assuming. The work that follows, whether that means a workshop, a briefing, focused analysis, or an ongoing engagement, is built around that.

Being small keeps the work direct. The person developing the approach is the same person doing it.
